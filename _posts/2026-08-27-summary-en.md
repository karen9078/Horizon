The FDA has approved the first-in-class targeted therapy for metastatic pancreatic cancer, marking a breakthrough in treating a previously 'undruggable' cancer target. This approval specifically targets KRAS mutations, which are present in a significant fraction of pancreatic cancers. This approval is significant because pancreatic cancer has historically had poor outcomes, and this new therapy offers a targeted option for patients with KRAS-mutated tumors. The approval was notably fast, with the FDA accepting the new drug application and approving it in just over a month, enabled by the FDA's CNPV Pilot Program. This contrasts with typical review timelines of 8-12 months for priority and standard reviews.

This new therapy represents a class of RAS inhibitors that can effectively target KRAS mutations, overcoming a long-standing challenge in oncology.

rss · Latent Space · Aug 26, 15:15

A Physics Foundation Model (PFM) would apply this paradigm to physical simulations, potentially eliminating the need for specialized solver development and enabling reasoning across diverse physical systems.
