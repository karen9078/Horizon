## [Bug Blindness: Why Developers and Users Miss Obvious Bugs](https://danluu.com/bug-blind/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Dan Luu's article 'Bug Blindness' explores the cognitive phenomenon where developers and users fail to notice obvious bugs due to mental model alignment, normalization of deviance, and other factors. The piece has gained significant traction on Hacker News with 198 points and 96 comments. This article sheds light on a subtle but critical aspect of software quality that affects both developers and end-users. Understanding bug blindness can help teams improve debugging practices and user experience by recognizing and mitigating these cognitive biases. The article discusses how developers' mental models can be so aligned with the system that they share the same blind spots, while users may not notice bugs due to normalization of deviance or lack of mental models. It also highlights that even when users report issues, they may not be recognized as bugs by developers.

hackernews · davidmckenna · Aug 30, 00:21 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49494520)

**Background**: Bug blindness is a cognitive bias where individuals fail to perceive obvious defects due to familiarity, mental models, or normalization of deviance. In software development, this can lead to persistent bugs that go unnoticed by both developers and users. The concept is related to other bug types like heisenbugs and schrödinbugs, which are documented in software engineering literature.

The DHS budget is substantial, and critics argue such actions represent an abuse of power.

California lawmakers unanimously passed a law exempting open-source software distributed under GPL, MIT, BSD, and Apache licenses from age-verification requirements. This marks a significant legal victory for the Linux and open-source community. This exemption sets a precedent for how governments regulate open-source software, potentially influencing other states and countries. It ensures that Linux and other open-source operating systems can be used without mandatory age verification, which could have hindered their adoption. The law specifically exempts software distributed under GPL, MIT, BSD, and Apache licenses, but does not provide a specific list of open licenses; instead, it describes what constitutes open software. There remains a grey area regarding whether Valve's SteamOS, which is based on Linux but may include proprietary components, qualifies for the exemption.

**Background**: Age-verification laws in California and Colorado require operating systems to implement device-level age verification to protect minors online. Open-source software, by its nature, is often developed and distributed by communities without centralized control, making compliance with such mandates difficult. The exemption recognizes the unique characteristics of open-source projects, which are typically transparent and community-driven.
