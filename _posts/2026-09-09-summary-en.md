## [OpenAI Claims AI Proof of Navier-Stokes Singularity, Sparking Debate](https://openai.com/index/navier-stokes-solution/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

OpenAI announced that an internal AI system has produced a proof showing that the Navier-Stokes equations can develop a singularity in finite time, addressing one of the Millennium Prize Problems. The company shared both a writeup of the proof and a formalization in the Lean proof assistant. If validated, this would be the first AI-generated solution to a Millennium Prize Problem, potentially transforming how mathematical research is conducted. However, the claim has ignited intense debate about the validity of AI proofs, research ethics, and the future of collaborative mathematics. The proof claims that the dynamics of the Navier-Stokes equations can develop a singularity in finite time, which contradicts the expected smoothness for certain initial conditions. OpenAI states the model was trained for less than two weeks and is reportedly more than twice as capable in mathematics as its recently released Astra model, though this claim is unverified.

hackernews · OpenAI News · Sep 8, 17:13 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49613262)

**Background**: The Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem is one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems posed by the Clay Mathematics Institute in 2000, each carrying a $1 million prize. It asks whether solutions to the Navier-Stokes equations, which describe fluid motion, always exist and remain smooth, or whether they can develop singularities. As of 2026, only the Poincaré conjecture has been officially solved. AI-driven mathematical research is an emerging field, with tools like Lean being used to formalize proofs.
