## [Signal to Enable Phone-Number-Free Registration via Zero-Knowledge Proofs](https://community.signalusers.org/t/registration-without-a-phone-number/2222?page=10) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Signal is planning to allow users to register without a phone number by using zero-knowledge proofs, according to community discussion and code commits. The implementation may require a purchase through Google Play Billing to mitigate spam while keeping SMS verification as an option. This move represents a significant privacy advancement for a widely-used secure messaging app, as it could decouple identity from phone numbers and reduce metadata exposure. It may influence other messaging platforms to adopt similar privacy-preserving registration methods. The zero-knowledge proof would allow a user to prove they possess a valid credential without revealing it, but the exact mechanism and whether it fully prevents spam remain unclear. Community members also noted that the release cycle now supports Android tablets without a SIM as first-class adjunct devices.

hackernews · Cider9986 · Sep 13, 21:47 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49689048)

**Background**: Signal is a messaging app known for its strong end-to-end encryption, but historically required a phone number for registration, which ties accounts to real-world identities. Zero-knowledge proofs are cryptographic protocols that let one party prove a statement is true without revealing any underlying information, and they are increasingly used in privacy-focused systems to verify credentials anonymously.

## [Cars Are Collecting and Selling Driver Data to Third Parties](https://www.theverge.com/column/994172/your-car-is-selling-your-data) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A Verge column details how modern connected cars collect driver data—including location, speed, and biometric information—and sell it to third parties, prompting a robust Hacker News discussion on privacy and regulation. Commenters highlighted personal experiences with data collection and pointed to California's AB-1542, which would ban the sale of sensitive geolocation data. This issue affects millions of drivers who may be unaware that their vehicle data is being monetized, raising significant privacy and consumer protection concerns. It also signals a growing regulatory focus on automotive data, with laws like AB-1542 potentially reshaping how automakers and data brokers operate. The data collected includes sensitive categories such as geolocation accurate to within 1,850 feet, which AB-1542 classifies as sensitive personal information. Even when drivers disable data collection in companion apps, some data may still be shared, and opting out often does not delete previously collected data.

hackernews · bookofjoe · Sep 13, 13:45 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49683953)

**Background**: Connected cars are equipped with sensors and internet connectivity that enable features like navigation and remote unlocking, but they also continuously gather data on driving behavior, location, and even biometrics. This data can be sold to third parties for purposes such as insurance risk assessment, marketing, and urban planning, often without explicit consumer consent. The Federal Trade Commission has warned that such collection and use may violate existing laws, and regulations like California's AB-1542 aim to restrict the sale of sensitive data.

## [Fable 5.1 Solves 370-Year-Old Cyphral Distich Cipher](https://www.vals.ai/blogs/fable-solves-cyphral-distich) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Vals AI announced that Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.1 autonomously solved the Cyphral Distich, a 64-number cryptogram printed at the end of Sir Thomas Urquhart's 1653 book Logopandecteision, in 44 minutes and roughly 176,000 tokens with zero human intervention. The model found a key embedded in the text itself, cracking a cipher that had remained unread for 370 years. This result highlights how LLMs can automate the kind of tedious searching, testing, and reference-tracing that historically bottlenecked cryptographic and scholarly work, potentially unlocking many neglected historical puzzles. It also fuels the broader debate over whether such wins reflect genuine general reasoning ability or simply abundant low-hanging fruit that few humans ever bothered to examine. The Cyphral Distich is a cryptogram of two lines of 32 numbers each, and the key needed to decode it was embedded within the text itself rather than supplied externally. The solve took about 44 minutes and roughly 176,000 tokens, and Vals AI framed the task as an open-ended goal: find an unsolved historical cipher and crack it.

hackernews · u1hcw9nx · Sep 13, 21:06 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49688695)

**Background**: The Cyphral Distich appears at the end of Logopandecteision, a 1653 book by the Scottish polymath Sir Thomas Urquhart, who also proposed a universal language. A cryptogram is a short message deliberately encoded so it cannot be read without knowing the rule that produced it, and this particular one had resisted decoding for centuries. Claude Fable 5.1 is an Anthropic LLM positioned as an improvement over Fable 5, with gains in agentic coding, long-running agentic workflows, and knowledge work.

## [OpenAI Opens Codex Harness via Agents API Public Beta](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiiwFBVV95cUxNZE5ZTEp5ZzIzYkpXVjZnLVRnandLRnNHV0t6SnpnUU5Bd2lja0w5bVRwRkNwWnhna0I0TVk4MXhOcTZyV0Fqcmtqb1hoYVJQaExsbzdPeEJJN2RmTE43cnBsUkZBOGM2M0FVSEFCSmJxaEFpeERLVndfU0FYVVZFalRrdGh2WDZDdW9N?oc=5) ⭐️ 7.0/10

OpenAI launched a public beta of its Agents API on September 10, 2026, exposing the managed Codex harness to all developers. The API lets applications build and run long-running cloud agents with session orchestration, context compaction, tool use, and multi-agent delegation behind a single interface. This lowers the barrier for teams that have moved beyond single model calls but don't want to build and maintain their own agent runtime. By exposing the same infrastructure that powers Codex and ChatGPT for Work, OpenAI is positioning itself as the managed backend for autonomous coding and workflow agents, intensifying competition with other agent frameworks. The managed harness provides durable sessions, sandboxed execution environments, MCP tool access, delegated subagents, context compaction, failure recovery, and streamed events. It is powered by the open-source Codex harness, so developers can inspect the core logic that coordinates model calls, tools, and context, while OpenAI operates and maintains it.

google_news · The Eastern Herald · Sep 13, 09:03

**Background**: An agent harness is the orchestration layer that manages how an AI model calls tools, keeps context across steps, and recovers from failures during long tasks. Previously, developers building such agents had to assemble these pieces themselves using frameworks like the Agents SDK. The Agents API packages that runtime as a managed cloud service, similar to how cloud platforms abstract away server management.
